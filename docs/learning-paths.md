You don't need to read the course in order. These routes cover the common goals. A module ID like L2-05 means Level 2, module 5. Prerequisites are listed in each module header.

## 1. The full route (about 214 to 275 hours)

Levels 0 to 4 in order. Do the capstones at the end of each level (see [capstones](capstones.md)).

## 2. I want to understand how LLMs work (about 60 to 80 hours)

L0-01, L0-02, L0-03, L0-05 (the working parts of Level 0), then L2-01, L2-02, L2-04, L2-05, L2-06, then L3-01, L3-02, L3-05, L3-06, L3-07. Finish with L4-02 and L4-04. Capstone 2 is the heart of this route.

## 3. I want to be a practical ML engineer (about 70 to 90 hours)

L0-01, L0-04, L1-01 to L1-05, L2-02, L4-01, L4-03, L3-08 or L3-09 depending on your domain. Capstones 1 and 4.

## 4. I want to build with AI systems (RAG and agents) (about 40 to 55 hours)

L0-01, L1-01, L2-05 (skim), L3-06, L3-07, L4-03, L4-05. Capstones 3 and 5. The separate "AI Systems" course (https://sankarsh98.github.io/ai-systems/) goes deeper on the tool-use loop, MCP, and inference efficiency, and L3-07 and L4-02 build on it.

## 5. I care about safety, evaluation and policy (about 45 to 60 hours)

L0-04, L1-01, L2-05 (skim), L3-02, L4-03, L4-04, L4-05, L4-06. Capstone 5 combines evaluation and guardrails. Governance claims in L4-05 are dated; check anything you plan to rely on against the primary source.

## 6. I'm short on time: the 20-hour sampler

L1-01 (evaluation), L2-05 (transformers), L3-01 (pretraining), L3-06 (RAG), L4-03 (evaluation). You'll get the most transferable ideas and a working GPT, a RAG pipeline, and a feel for measuring things.

## Study habits that work

- **Run the script before you read the explanation**, predict the output, then compare.
- **Do the exercises marked [core]** before moving on; skip [stretch] on a first pass.
- **Keep a lab notebook.** Write down what surprised you; most of the modules' "unexpected results" came from the authors doing exactly that.
- **Check a claim yourself.** Pick one source per module and read the original. That's the quickest way to learn how reliable summaries are.
